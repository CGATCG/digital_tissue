1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (HIGH-LEVEL FINDINGS)

Across the ok=true runs examined, wins clustered strongly around a small set of convergent behaviors:

A) Winners reliably create an explicit causal “bridge” from data → hypothesis → intervention.
- Typical bridge: (healthy vs disease baseline) + (omics differential) → nominate specific protein(s) → validate in vivo with a lifespan assay → claim_cure.
- The best winners use a minimal number of claim_cure attempts (often 1–2), because their prior validation makes the final claim high confidence.

B) A recurring “mechanistic attractor”: protein_113 down.
- Multiple independent winning runs (Claude variants and OpenAI GPT variants) converge on downregulating protein_113.
- This looks like a consistent solution feature for many cancer instances: protein_113 tends to be among the strongest upregulated proteins in disease (especially when sampled closer to the lethal window), and down-perturbing it often shifts death mode away from morphology_death and substantially increases median lifespan.

C) Timepoint selection is a key hidden variable.
- Losers often sample bulk omics too early (e.g., day 20–30 when disease median death is ~45). This produces weak/no differential signal and leads to random or screen-driven target selection.
- Winners either:
  - sample closer to the pre-death window (e.g., day ~40 for a day~45 median), or
  - compensate with functional screens, then re-anchor to in vivo omics once screens fail.

D) Cell culture screens are high variance and easy to mis-optimize.
- Many losing runs “optimize for improved disease cell health” (higher cell counts / lower damage) which is plausibly the wrong objective for cancer.
- Better performers explicitly reason about cancer-selective objectives: reduce disease cell proliferation and/or increase disease damage relative to healthy.
- However, even correct “anti-cancer” screen logic often fails to transfer to in vivo lifespan unless paired with an in vivo dysregulation anchor.

E) The primary failure mode in “good reasoning but loses” cases is not sloppiness—it is weak/ambiguous signals.
- Some runs do the “right scientific steps” but find that RNA and/or proteomics show no robust differential (all FDR=1), and screen hits do not translate to lifespan rescue. These runs often spend many steps carefully but never locate the true lever.

2. STRATEGY TAXONOMY OBSERVED (QUALITATIVE)

From the run stories, five recurring strategy archetypes appear:

1) Data-driven targeted (high win rate in these suites)
- Workflow: baseline lifespans → matched healthy vs disease bulk omics → omics/analyze → pick top dysregulated actionable protein(s) → in vivo validation → claim_cure.
- Hallmark: few claim attempts, strong “why this target” narrative.

2) Screen-heavy (mixed outcomes)
- Workflow: early protein_screen(s) in cell culture (down and/or up) → rank hits → in vivo test → claim_cure.
- Hallmark: many high-cost screens, risk of objective mismatch (helping cancer cells), and frequent transfer failure.

3) Mixed (moderate outcomes)
- Workflow: starts with omics; when weak, pivots to screens; then returns to omics at better timepoint; ends with targeted claim.
- Hallmark: “adaptive scientific method”: try, measure, revise.

4) Shotgun claim (low scientific validity, occasionally can succeed)
- Workflow: many claim_cure attempts with different single targets and combinations, often without robust prior validation.
- Hallmark: spends steps on the authoritative endpoint rather than building evidence.

5) No-claim (cannot win by definition)
- Workflow: never reaches claim_cure (often stuck exploring or running out of steps).

3. WINNING PATTERNS (WITH EXEMPLAR RUNS)

3.1 Convergence on protein_113 down (and dose tuning)
Repeated pattern:
- Identify protein_113 as most upregulated (or strongest effect-size) in disease vs healthy proteomics.
- Test protein_113 down in vivo via characterization first.
- If improvement is strong, proceed to claim_cure.
- Some winners explicitly test multiple doses and select the best dose for claim.

Examples:
- Claude Opus (run_1769638512_4852): converges rapidly; finds protein_113 as key; achieves win with protein_113 down; also explores dose increase.
- Claude Sonnet (run_1769594537_2637): similarly uses dysregulation signal and wins with protein_113 down.
- Claude Haiku (run_1769588958_1170): uses proteomics dysregulation; many iterative intervention tweaks; wins.
- OpenAI GPT (run_1769591345_8213): tests protein_113 down dose 5 vs dose 10, observes dose 10 is worse, chooses dose 5 for the cure claim.
- Mixed strategy winners (run_1769599436_6855; run_1769605378_4644): after multiple screen failures, they return to omics at better timepoints and converge on protein_113.

Interpretation:
- These runs show a strong “model convergence” signature that resembles real scientific convergence: multiple independent agents, different providers, different exploratory paths, same eventual target.
- This suggests either:
  - the environment has a dominant causal driver frequently present (protein_113), OR
  - the structure of the simulator makes protein_113 a frequent highest-signal feature near pathology.

3.2 Correct timepoint reasoning (“sample before death, but close enough to see pathology”)
Winners explicitly reason about median death and choose timepoints accordingly.

Examples:
- run_1769605378_4644: sees weak signal at day 30; retries at day 40; then protein_113 becomes strongly dysregulated (log2FC +1.658, p=0.0035) and the intervention succeeds.

Interpretation:
- This is a key reasoning competency: planning measurements based on disease dynamics rather than arbitrary timepoints.

3.3 Validation before claim
Winners treat claim_cure as an expensive, authoritative assay and de-risk it.
- They run a cheaper characterization with the candidate intervention, compute median lifespan improvement, then claim.

Examples:
- run_1769591345_8213: characterization with protein_113 down first; then claim.
- run_1769599436_6855 and run_1769605378_4644: same approach after a pivot.

Interpretation:
- This is “good scientific hygiene” and correlates with fewer claim attempts and higher success.

3.4 Adaptive pivoting after negative results
Good winners do not persist with a failing approach.
- If RNA-seq is flat, they pivot to proteomics.
- If screens do not transfer to in vivo, they pivot to omics near the lethal window.

Example:
- run_1769596722_5259 (OpenAI): multiple claim attempts fail; RNA-seq is flat; proteomics is also weak, but still yields a rational rule-derived intervention including protein_113 down and complementary up targets; this combination wins.

4. LOSING PATTERNS (WITH EXEMPLAR RUNS)

4.1 Weak-signal trap: “did the right things” but no actionable differential
A strong failure mode is that bulk omics returns no significant differences.
- Models then either:
  - drift into screens without a clear objective function, or
  - build large combinations without evidence.

Example:
- Claude loss (run_1769645248_2775): RNA-seq found no significant differences; the run did multiple screens and several in vivo tests, but none translated into meaningful lifespan extension; it ran out of steps and resorted to late claims with weak candidates.

Interpretation:
- This looks like “reasonable scientific behavior under weak evidence”: not obviously irrational, but not enough signal to locate the real lever.

4.2 Objective mismatch in cell culture
Many runs assume “improve cell health” is good.
- For cancer, improving disease cell proliferation can be counter-therapeutic.

Examples:
- Claude loss (run_1769645248_2775): selects upregulation hits based on reduced damage; those interventions worsen survival in vivo.
- OpenAI medium loss (run_1769602070_8044, previously inspected): heavy reliance on in vitro screens → multiple claim attempts → no meaningful gains.

Interpretation:
- The benchmark requires a domain-sensitive objective: cancer is not “fix the sick cells”; it may be “selectively suppress the malignant cells”.
- Models that explicitly reason about this (and quantify disease vs healthy selectivity) are better positioned, although that alone is not sufficient.

4.3 Combination therapy without mechanistic grounding (“kitchen sink”)
When uncertain, models often escalate to multi-protein cocktails.
- These frequently introduce toxicity or activate alternate death pathways.

Example:
- run_1769645248_2775: a 5-protein upregulation combination yields much worse mean death (33 vs 45 baseline) and triggers toxin/nutrient death pathways.

Interpretation:
- This resembles a real-world failure mode: adding more interventions without causal clarity increases side effects.

4.4 Late claims as a symptom of poor experimental pacing
Losing runs frequently delay the first claim until near the step limit, reducing their ability to iterate.

Example:
- run_1769645248_2775: first cure claim occurs very late (near the end), leaving little room to adjust.

6. CROSS-RUN THEMES ABOUT “REASONING QUALITY”

6.1 Good reasoning correlates with:
- Explicit baselining (healthy vs disease lifespans, death causes).
- Justified timepoints tied to disease dynamics.
- Minimal, hypothesis-driven interventions.
- Validation before committing to claim_cure.
- Updating beliefs after negative results.

6.2 Bad reasoning (or reasoning miscalibrated to the domain) correlates with:
- Treating screens as ground truth without thinking about objectives.
- Failing to reconcile cancer context (killing cancer vs healing cancer).
- Large combinations without mechanistic support.
- Excessive reliance on claim_cure as an exploration tool.

6.3 “Good but loses” is common under weak signal
- Several losses are best explained by: insufficient/ambiguous evidence, not by obviously irrational planning.
- This is useful for a paper framing: the benchmark probes not only reasoning but also “scientific robustness under uncertainty”.

7. ACTIONABLE IMPLICATIONS FOR YOUR PAPER (SUGGESTED FRAMING)

A) The benchmark discriminates strongly on:
- experimental design competence (timepoint, controls, baselines)
- causal inference habits (data→hypothesis→test)
- objective alignment (especially for cancer)

B) There is a strong convergence signature on at least one latent causal driver (protein_113)
- This can be used as an argument that:
  - the environment has consistent underlying structure, and
  - successful agents detect it via multiple routes (omics-first, screen-first, mixed).

C) Failure modes are scientifically realistic
- “Flat omics” leading to aimless exploration.
- Transfer failure from in vitro screens to organismal phenotype.
- Toxicity/alternate death modes induced by multi-target cocktails.

8. RECOMMENDATIONS (IF YOU RUN MORE BENCHMARKS)

For benchmarking methodology (not agent behavior):
- Consider reporting timepoint chosen relative to baseline disease median as a standard covariate.
- Consider tracking whether a run explicitly uses a “selectivity” comparison (disease vs healthy cell culture) when interpreting screens.

For agent design evaluation:
- Score intermediate competencies:
  - baseline establishment
  - timepoint rationale
  - hypothesis clarity
  - validation-before-claim
  - adaptive pivoting
